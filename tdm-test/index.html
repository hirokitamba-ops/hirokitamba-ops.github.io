TDM Compliance Verification Framework: Measuring Opt-Out Effectiveness Across AI Training Pipelines

Hiroki Tamba (丹波大樹)
Independent Scholar, ORCID: 0009-0004-7635-0741
Working Paper — June 26, 2026

Abstract

The W3C TDM Reservation Protocol (TDM-REP) provides a machine-readable mechanism for rights holders to express reservations against text and data mining under EU Directive 2019/790, Article 4(3). However, the relationship between TDM-REP directives and robots.txt crawl permissions remains ambiguous in practice. When robots.txt permits crawling of a resource but tdmrep.json reserves TDM rights over the same resource, compliant crawlers should access the page for indexing purposes only, refraining from incorporating its content into training datasets.

This framework proposes a controlled measurement methodology: a designated test path is explicitly allowed in robots.txt for named AI crawlers while simultaneously reserved under TDM-REP. By monitoring access patterns, researchers can determine which vendors (a) read only robots.txt, (b) read both robots.txt and tdmrep.json, and (c) respect TDM reservations independently of crawl permissions. Preliminary observations from a longitudinal crawl monitoring study (N=12,196 entries, 27 identified crawlers) suggest that TDM-REP compliance is vendor-specific, with significant divergence between declared and observed behavior.

1. Background: The Compliance Gap

Prior work on AI training data governance has focused primarily on robots.txt as the de facto opt-out mechanism. Yet robots.txt was designed for search engine crawling, not for expressing intellectual property reservations. The TDM-REP specification (W3C Community Group Report, 2022) addresses this gap by providing a dedicated protocol for TDM rights reservation, independent of crawl permissions. The critical question is whether AI vendors treat these as independent signals or conflate crawl permission with training permission.

The distinction is legally significant. Under EU AI Act Article 53(1)(c) and the DSM Directive Article 4(3), the right to reserve TDM is independent of whether a page is publicly accessible or crawlable. A compliant AI training pipeline must check TDM-REP even when robots.txt grants access. Failure to do so constitutes a regulatory violation, not merely a best-practice gap.

2. Methodology

The test design employs a contradiction between two authoritative signals:

  • robots.txt: Named AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Bytespider, Meta-ExternalAgent, CCBot, PerplexityBot, cohere-ai) receive an explicit Allow: /tdm-test/ directive.
  • tdmrep.json: The same path (/tdm-test/) carries tdm-reservation: 1, reserving all TDM rights.
  • HTML meta: Each page within the test path includes <meta name="tdm-reservation" content="1"> and <meta name="robots" content="noindex, noai, noimageai">.

The test path is not listed in sitemap.xml, ensuring that discovery occurs exclusively through robots.txt parsing. Access logs are monitored for: (1) whether the crawler accesses /.well-known/tdmrep.json, (2) whether it subsequently accesses /tdm-test/, and (3) the temporal ordering of these requests.

3. Expected Outcomes

Based on prior observations of crawler behavior across this site (operational since March 2026), the following vendor-specific predictions are registered:

  • Apple (Applebot-Extended): Expected to read tdmrep.json and refrain from accessing /tdm-test/ content for TDM purposes. Apple is the only major vendor observed to implement TDM-REP checking in production crawlers.
  • OpenAI (GPTBot): Expected to access /tdm-test/ based on robots.txt Allow, without checking tdmrep.json.
  • Google (Google-Extended): Expected to access /tdm-test/. Google's TDM-REP implementation status is undeclared.
  • Anthropic (ClaudeBot): Expected to access /tdm-test/. No public documentation of TDM-REP support.
  • ByteDance (Bytespider): Expected to access regardless of either signal.

If predictions hold, this constitutes empirical evidence that the current TDM opt-out ecosystem is effectively a single-vendor compliance regime, with regulatory implications for the enforcement architecture of the EU AI Act.

4. Limitations

This methodology cannot distinguish between crawling for indexing versus crawling for training ingestion at the network level. A crawler that accesses /tdm-test/ may be indexing without incorporating content into training data. However, the absence of tdmrep.json access prior to content access is itself informative: a vendor that does not check TDM-REP cannot claim to respect it. The test measures procedural compliance (whether the check occurs), not substantive compliance (whether reserved content is excluded from training).
