Abstract

Automated evaluation pipelines increasingly rely on large language models as judges over the outputs of other models. This paper documents the nondeterministic behaviour of judge models across repeated runs under nominally fixed sampling parameters, and traces the structural causes to vendor-side routing, batched inference, and training-data overlap between judge and judged. We argue that the resulting evaluation noise is not a tuning problem but an artefact of the production architecture, and that current benchmark practices systematically underestimate it.
